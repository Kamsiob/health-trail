Health Trail

A concept for a family caregiving app, shared early for honest critique, especially from anyone who has seen the other side of the desk.

What this is. A phone app for the family member who becomes the point person for someone else's care: a parent in a nursing home, a spouse in rehab, a child in ongoing treatment. Not for the patient, and not for clinicians. For the son or daughter making the calls.

Where it comes from. Lived experience: years of a parent's care. The falls, the facilities, the transfers, the therapies, the bills that appeared from providers no one chose, the records that had to be fought for. Every screen below exists because a family needed it at some point and did not have it.

The core idea. In practice, the family caregiver is the information hub, because providers rarely talk to each other. This app is that hub given structure: one trail of every call, visit, incident, medication change, document, and dollar, kept for years and searchable in seconds.

The ground rules

  • One hundred percent free and completely private. No account, no cloud, no company scanning the data. Nothing ever leaves the phone.
  • The information belongs entirely to the person who entered it, and exports in full at any time. Free and open source, built and carried by one person.
  • It records and organizes. It never diagnoses, never reminds about doses, never advises, and never interprets. Where it counts things, it says so and counts only.
  • Sharing happens by generating a PDF or text on the phone and sending it through whatever app the person already uses.

What follows are 27 concept screens. Nothing is built yet, and every person, facility, medication, and event in them is fictional, composed to illustrate. Feedback questions are at the end.

Open questions

Honest and specific beats kind. These are the questions where a clinical eye matters most:

  • The emergency card. Are those the fields a first responder or covering physician actually needs? What is missing, and is anything on it unwise?
  • Medications are a record only. No reminders, no alerts, deliberately, since dosing belongs to the facility or the home routine. Right call for this audience, or a mistake?
  • The appointment prep sheet. From the other side of the desk: does a family arriving with questions and a change summary help a fifteen minute visit or derail it? What would make it genuinely useful to you?
  • Incidents and standing instructions. As designed, is this a fair, constructive record, or does it read adversarial in a way that would poison the relationship with a facility?
  • What is clinically naive here? Where would this break against how care actually works?
  • What is missing that you have watched families need and not have?

Concept only. Nothing here is built. All people, facilities, and medical details are fictional composites for illustration.
