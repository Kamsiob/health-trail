Health Trail · Today · Proposal Grid v1

A desk, set the way you keep it

A redesign of the Today tab as a modular dashboard of cards, the way a person arranges a desk or a phone home screen: what they check first sits where their eyes land, and everything on it is theirs to add, move, resize, or remove. It has to be visual without being decorative, so every card earns its place by answering a real recurring question from the record, deterministically, with no interpretation and no advice. Everything stays inside design direction v4: the five laws, the six costumes, the tokens, the field notebook. The user-facing word is cards, because in this binder they are index cards; this document says card throughout and means the same thing a launcher means by widget.

PART A

The thinking

Six panels. The first one resolves the real tension in this brief, so it comes first.

Law 1 versus modularity, resolved

A free-form dashboard breaks the first law: if the person can stack six equal cards, no screen has one thing first. The resolution is a fixed structure with free contents. Today always has exactly one lead slot at the top, at display scale, singular by construction. Below it sits the card field, then the folds. What fills the lead is the person's choice: by default it is the digest, the deterministic sentence about today drawn from the record, and any card can be promoted to lead from its options, which demotes the previous lead back into the field. There is never zero and never two. Customization decides what deserves the top; the law decides that something singular is at the top. The universal search bar and the gold capture button keep their places on Today regardless of layout, because finding and recording are the two acts that must never move.

What a card is

A card is a named, deterministic question asked of the record every time Today opens: what is the next dated thing, how many are open, what was the latest value, who was seen last. Nothing more. The answer renders; the card is a door to where the answer lives. This is the whole pull model: every card declares one query over the same single database, queries run on open and after any save, everything is local, and no card ever computes an interpretation, a trend judgment, a recommendation, or a nudge. The engine that writes the digest sentence is the same deterministic template engine used everywhere else. Anatomy, uniform across all cards: a small index tab in the card's section hue and wash, naming its section in mono; the answer, sized by the card; a source or time line in quiet type; and a corner chevron, because every card is a door. Identity comes from the tab pack, never from decoration: an appointments card is slate because appointments are slate everywhere in the binder. Cards for whole-app surfaces, projects and the trail and the digest, wear gold.

Three sizes, and how content adapts

Platform widget research is consistent and this design follows it: small carries one fact and is one touch target; wide carries a short list or a fact with one inline action, at most two targets; tall carries a chart or a small feed, at most three targets. Every card supports at least two sizes and declares its behavior at each. Growing a card never adds a new kind of content, it reveals more of the same answer: the medications card at small is a count, at wide it is the list. Shrinking never hides the existence of something open, only its detail. Inline actions appear only at wide and tall, always outlined, always a verb or a dialable number.

Small Half width. One answer, one line of context. The whole card is the touch target.
Wide Full width. The answer plus two or three lines, or one inline outlined action beside it.
Tall Full width, taller. A chart drawn under the chart card's rules, a mini spine, or a short list. Never a dense feed.

The states ladder: how a card changes with its data

A card's look is driven by what the record holds, and every card must define its whole ladder before it ships. This is where visual honesty lives, and it is the difference between useful and decorative.

Full The normal render. Real values, real dates, tabular numerals.
Few Sparse-data grace applies. A measure with two entries draws two dots and says "2 entries so far." No line is invented between points that are far apart in time; gaps stay gaps.
None yet "Not yet" is a calm state, never an error and never a scold. The card names its one action: "Nothing scheduled. Add one." Quiet is allowed to be good news, and the card says so plainly: "Nothing waiting."
Passed A date that has gone by reads "passed 6 days ago" in plain words. No urgency color, no alarm. The semantic colors keep their locked meanings; alert appears on exactly one card, open incidents, because that is alert's meaning.
Source closed A card pointing at a closed project or an ended thread says so: "This project closed Nov 8." It keeps working as a door to the record and is removed only by the person's hand. The app never deletes a card on its own.
Returning After a long absence the digest leads with "since you were last here" and plain counts, and every card simply shows its current true answer. No card ever measures the person's absence back at them.

Defaults beat blank canvases

Choice research is blunt: a person facing a blank dashboard and forty options builds nothing. So nobody ever sees a blank Today. The situation template chosen at onboarding ships a complete starting layout, which is Law 5 doing the work: home care leads with the digest and carries medications, next up, a measure, and questions; a rehab stay carries progress and milestones high; a hospital stay carries next up, questions, and incidents; a coming-home project puts its steps and its date on Today from day one. The catalog itself is curated, roughly sixteen cards, one per real recurring question, not one per table. And two rules keep control where it belongs: the app never rearranges Today on its own, ever, and every template default is just a starting hand, editable from the first minute without penalty. Personalization here is the person's explicit choice plus their stated situation. It is never inference, because this app does not watch its user.

The people this must hold

The daily manager Home care, years in. Opens Today four times a day for the same three answers: medications, next appointment, the weight chart. Her Today is dense, wide cards, chart on top as lead.
The hospital-week point person Everything is changing daily. Needs next up, questions to ask on rounds, and open incidents where they cannot be missed. Will rearrange twice in a week, and that has to cost seconds.
The parent of a child in treatment Lives between appointments and approvals. Next up as lead, the project's next date beside it, capture always one thumb away. The voice rules from the Projects grid govern here too.
The one bringing a parent home A project's steps and its one converging date, promoted onto Today for two intense weeks, then removed when life settles. Cards that come and go with seasons are the system working, not churn.
The returner Four months away. The digest says what the file held, the cards show true current answers, and nothing shames. One tap from the digest resumes.
The one who never customizes Most people, per every launcher study. The template default must be genuinely good, because it is the only Today this person will ever see. The system is judged by its defaults, not its options.

PART D

How it fits the system

The rules that make this buildable and keep it honest, plus what waits on the owner.

Mechanics, for the builder

The layout is data: an ordered list of card instances, each with a type, a size, and an optional source id (which measure, which project, which person), plus one lead assignment. It lives in the same database, travels in the archive, and restores on import like everything else; a person's arranged Today survives a phone migration. Situation templates ship a default layout as part of their bundle, copied at onboarding with no live link, same rule as project templates. Each card type declares one deterministic query and its full states ladder; queries run when Today gains focus and after any save, reading the same SQLite as every screen. A card whose source id no longer resolves renders its source-closed state and is never auto-removed. Reordering is drag with a Move up and Move down path in card options, so it works one-handed, with TalkBack, and with switch access. Edit mode is entered by the visible Edit button; touch and hold on the field is a shortcut only. The in-app Today and the OS home screen widgets are separate systems: the existing OS widget commitments (capture, and later possibly a small subset of these cards) are unchanged by this design, and nothing here assumes them.

Costume audit, pre-run

The card In costume terms every card is a row ending in a chevron: one door, opening its section or item. Its tab, numbers, and chart are bare. Inline actions exist only at wide and tall, always outlined pills, always a verb or a dialable number, never more than the size's target budget: one at small (the card itself), two at wide, three at tall.
The lead slot The one-thing hero in costume. Bare except for any outlined action its card defines.
Edit mode The remove dot and drag handle exist only inside edit mode, which is a modal state entered by a visible button, keeping "nothing bare responds to touch" true in normal reading. Size choices and reorder buttons are chips and outlined pills in the options sheet.
Add a card A sheet of rows with outlined Add actions, grouped by section, previews rendered with real data.

The laws, checked

Law 1 The lead slot is singular by construction. The field never contains a second display-scale element; card titles cap at the bt size.
Law 2 Every card is one door. Nothing bare responds. The audit runs per card type per size, and a card fails the screen if any state in its ladder breaks costume.
Law 3 Card options ask one question per sheet. Adding is choose, then configure if needed, then done. Never a form.
Law 4 The states ladder is the empty-one-few-many rule applied per card. The field itself scales: many cards scroll; the folds and search remain reachable; pinning integrates, and pinning anything anywhere offers Add to Today.
Law 5 Templates ship whole starting hands. The catalog's suggested group is the situation's, and every default is editable from minute one.

What Today never does

It never rearranges itself, promotes a card, or injects one, no matter what the data does; the difference between screens 02 and 03 is data inside a layout the person owns, and that distinction is the whole trust model of the surface. It never interprets: no trends called good or bad, no streaks, no scores, no advice, no urgency theater. It never colors by value; the semantic colors keep their locked meanings, and the tab pack stays identity, never state. It never renders medical judgment, targets, or ranges on any chart, at any size. It never treats quiet as a failure to fix or absence as a debt to collect. And it never loses the arrangement: the layout is record, archived and restored like everything else the person has made.

Open questions for the owner

Catalog sign-off Seventeen card types across sixteen shelves in Part C. Approve, cut, or add; the catalog is the choice-overload defense, so additions should displace, not accumulate.
The digest when demoted When another card leads, the digest sentence lives as a wide field card by default. Alternative: it could merge into the header under the date. Preference?
Medications card scope Drawn here as list and count only, consistent with the undecided reminders question. If reminders are ever decided in, that is a new card conversation, not a change to this one.
Template default hands The four starting layouts (home care, hospital, rehab, coming home) need your ruling card by card, since most people will never change them. Drafted in this grid for home care and coming home; the other two follow the same logic and await your pass.
