Health Trail · Projects · Proposal Grid v2

Where it stands, and what comes next

A redesign of the Projects experience. A project is a long process being carried for someone you love: a waiver application, an insurance appeal, bringing a parent home, keeping a child's treatment covered. The person carrying it is tired, and the process is slow and scattered. This design gives it one calm place that always answers three things: where it stands, the next date that matters, and how it was left. Everything stays inside design direction v4: the five laws, the six costumes, the tokens, the field notebook. Projects is a whole-app surface, so it uses gold and the base ladder.

PART A

The thinking

Read this before the screens. The voice matters as much as the layout here, so the voice rules come first.

The voice: steady, never combative

The person opening this tab may be a parent whose child is in treatment, or a son trying to bring his mother home. Nothing in this surface may frame their situation as a battle, a game, or a race. Banned framings, in code and copy alike: fight, battle, win, lose, opponent, the ball, having the last word, and any sports or war metaphor. What replaces them is plain, steady language about a shared road: where it stands, since when, what was said, what comes next. The app is a companion holding the record so the person does not have to hold it in their head at 2 AM. Two more voice rules. First, other people in a process are named by role, never cast as adversaries; a caseworker is a caseworker. Second, urgency is stated as fact, never performed: a date is a number and its source, not a warning. The app records; the person decides.

Why the checklist could not carry this

In a long process, most of what happens is not a task the person can check off. They submitted the application; now the county is reviewing it, and has been for weeks. A checklist shows that person a wall of unchecked boxes and makes the waiting feel like their failure. Caregiver guidance from patient advocates and ombudsman programs is consistent about what actually carries families through: a complete record, every call with a name, a date, and a reference number, and no missed window. Most denials are reversed when families keep going, and the record is what makes keeping going possible. So the redesign is built around the three things a person needs the moment they open a project, in this order: where it stands, the next date that matters, and how it was left. The checklist remains, as steps, and leads only in the one shape where the work truly is many small arrangements.

The three answers, as structure

Where it stands Every active project always knows whose hands it is in and since when. "The county is reviewing the application. 23 days so far." "Waiting on Dr. Okafor's letter." "The next step is yours, when you are ready." The elapsed time is a fact drawn from entries, never a judgment. Updating it is a one-stage sheet, and logging a call offers the update automatically when things changed hands.
The next date Dates are recorded facts, each carrying its source: "From the letter of March 5." The next one that matters sits near the top of every active project as a countdown in tabular mono. No urgency colors; the semantic colors are locked, and the number plus its words carry the weight. A passed date reads "passed 6 days ago," plainly. The app never invents a date and never advises acting on one.
The latest word The most recent thing said by the office, the insurer, or the facility: who said it, when, and the reference number. It sits on the project home because it is what the person repeats at the start of every call: "On March 28, Denise, reference 4471-B, said it was with the nurse reviewer." One tap opens the entry it came from.

How templates shape a project, exactly

This was implicit before. It is now explicit, in the mechanics and on the screens. A project template is a bundle of five defaults, nothing more and nothing less, and every one of the five is visible, editable, and removable after setup:

1 · The stages The named stretches of the road, drawn as the road strip on the project home and the project card. Benefits: Applied, In review, Decision. Appeal: Decision received, Preparing, Submitted, Answered. Discharge: Date set, Arranging, Home. Stages can be renamed, added, or removed; the road redraws.
2 · The lead Which answer opens the screen: where it stands, the next date, or the steps. This is the "shape" of the project. The template sets it; screen 18 changes it, one control, no penalty.
3 · The starting steps A short editable list of what people in this situation usually need to gather or arrange. They are suggestions of structure, not instructions to act: every one can be deleted, and the app never says do this now.
4 · The usual papers Named placeholders in Papers, empty until filled: the application copy, the letters received, proof of what was sent. An empty placeholder reads "not yet," never as an error.
5 · The date types The kinds of dates this situation tends to have, offered as chips when a date is recorded, so a person photographing a letter can file its date in one tap.

The template is applied once, at setup, as a preview the person confirms (screen 04). After that there is no live link: editing a project never touches the template, and updating a template never touches existing projects. Whether the template library itself is static or opt-in updated from GitHub remains the owner's open decision and is not settled here.

Three shapes, one grammar

The long road, led by where it stands Benefits and waiver applications, guardianship, a placement waitlist. Months pass between events. Where it stands leads, the next date follows, the trail folds by stage, and the quiet stretches are drawn honestly: "23 days pass."
The closing window, led by the next date Appeals, disputes, anything with a filing deadline. The countdown leads. Directly beneath it, what the file still needs before that date, each item there or not yet. Where it stands follows.
The busy stretch, led by the steps Discharge planning, a move between facilities, getting the house ready. Two intense weeks of small parallel arrangements, often shared with siblings. The steps lead, clustered by area with an arranged count, each step able to carry who is handling it. The one date everything converges on sits above.

The people this must hold

The long-haul applicant Eighteen months into a waiver application, three caseworkers in. Needs the record to show the pattern, and the latest word to open every call with.
The parent of a child in treatment Coordinating approvals and coverage between hospital and insurer while living at the bedside. Needs capture that costs seconds and a file that never loses a reference number. The voice rules exist for her.
The one bringing a parent home A nursing home stay that needs to end well: a discharge date, equipment, the house made safe, the paperwork closed out. Needs the busy stretch shape and the shared steps.
The appeal filer Sixty days to respond to a decision, working nights. Needs the countdown and the list of what the file still needs, so nothing is discovered missing on day 58.
The returner Away for four months because life demanded it. Gets "since you were last here" in plain counts and one gentle way back in. Lapse tolerance is a law of this app.
The one who needs it on paper Hands the whole record to an attorney, an ombudsman, or a sibling as one PDF: every call, every letter, every date, in order. The project export is the assembled collection, surfaced where the process lives.

Connected, both ways, everywhere

A project is where a process is carried, not where its records are locked away. A call logged into a project is also on the notebook trail. A photographed bill in Money links to the project questioning it. A person appears in every project they touch, and their card says so through the "also in" row. Capture opened from inside a project arrives with the project pre-answered as a chosen chip, one tap to change. Every link is a door, and every door shows the way back.

What is proposed, and what is deliberately absent

Five compositions are proposed for the component inventory, each assembled from existing costumes and type rules, no new colors, no new interactive grammar: the road strip (the project's stages as a horizontal stretch of the trail, waypoints and dashed rail, full size on the project home and mini on its card), the standing card (where it stands: an eyebrow, a plain display sentence, the elapsed fact, and optional outlined actions), the date row (a tabular countdown beside its fact and source, opening its detail), the latest word card (a gold wash band quoting the most recent entry, with attribution and reference line, opening that entry), and the step row (a checklist line with an optional handler tag). The reference line, mono in a sand pill, becomes the standard dress for reference numbers everywhere. These require the owner's sign-off before build. Deliberately absent, and to stay absent: progress percentages, urgency colors, streaks or scores, advice about what to do next, and any language of winning, losing, or fighting.

PART C

How it fits the system

Nothing here escapes the grammar. This part maps the new surface onto the rules that govern every other screen, and states the template mechanics one more time in the builder's terms.

Template mechanics, for the builder

A project template is data, not behavior: a name, an ordered list of stage names, a lead (one of three), a list of starting steps, a list of paper placeholders, and a list of date kinds. Applying a template copies its contents into the new project at creation; there is no reference back, so template edits never touch existing projects and project edits never touch templates. The built-in templates ship bundled per the existing template library spec: Benefits or waiver, Insurance appeal, Discharge or move, and Blank, where Blank is an empty bundle with one stage and the lead set to where it stands. Users can duplicate and edit any template, per the existing three-tier library. Every template string follows the original wording rule and the "details vary by state" posture where applicable. The open decision on static versus opt-in updated templates is unchanged by this design.

Costume audit, pre-run on the proposed pieces

The road strip Bare. It is information, the project's stages drawn as trail, and it does nothing on touch. The stages are edited from Setup, screen 18. Mirrors fully in RTL.
The standing card A grouped surface. Its actions, dial and Update, are outlined pills, verbs or dialable numbers. The sentence itself is bare.
The date row A row ending in a chevron: opens the date's detail, which names its source paper. The number is data, mono, tabular, bare.
The latest word card A gold wash band; gold is the trail, and the latest word is a trail entry surfaced. Behaves as a row with a chevron, opening the entry it quotes.
The step row The checklist line. The box is the interactive element; the handler tag is data, mono, bare. Any swipe has a visible button path in the row's detail.

The laws, checked

Law 1 Each shape has exactly one hero: where it stands, the next date, or the steps. One filled action per screen; where the FAB is present, other primaries go outlined.
Law 2 Six costumes, nothing new. The five proposed pieces are compositions and need the owner's sign-off as inventory additions before build.
Law 3 Start a project, update where it stands, log a call, and the road turning are all staged sheets, three stages maximum, chips first, camera first for paper, skip and save always live.
Law 4 Project cards fold when on hold or closed. The project trail gains the scrubber and scoped search as it grows. Pins outrank. Empty, one, few, and many are drawn or mapped.
Law 5 The template sets the five defaults, previewed at creation and changeable from one screen forever after. The shape is a default, never a cage.

Depth and doors

Projects tab is level one. A project home is level two. Trail, papers, people, setup, and any entry are level three, and depth stops. Every add and edit is a sheet. Every named thing is a door with a way back: entry to project, project to person, person to their other projects, paper to the call that produced it. The "also in" row is a fold row in costume and the only new navigation idea.

What the app never does here

It never advises: it does not say file, call, escalate, or hurry. It never casts anyone as an adversary or the process as a fight. It records dates the person took from real papers, each showing its source, and states them as numbers, never as alarms. It never colors by urgency; the semantic colors keep their locked meanings. It never scores, streaks, or percents anything. It never treats a lapse as failure. And it never loses the record: everything in a project lives in the same single database, travels in the same archive, and renders in the same readable copy as the rest of the trail.

Open questions for the owner

Inventory sign-off The five proposed compositions, road strip included. Approve, amend, or strike; DESIGN.md records the ruling either way.
Handler tags A step can carry a name like Maria: a label only, no account, no notification, single point person model untouched. Confirm that reading.
The review-agency sentence Screen 15 carries the single factual line about what review agencies ask families for, per the earlier spec note. Confirm the wording or cut it.
Existing projects Checklist-era projects migrate with their checklist as one steps cluster, lead set to the steps, one stage named Underway, nothing lost, reshapeable from Setup. Confirm.
