Health Trail by Kamsiob · Design direction v4

Calm by default.
Built for the long haul.

The field-notebook identity stays: paper, ink, tabs, the trail. What changes is the experience architecture. Every screen now answers one question, shows one thing first, and folds the rest away until asked. And every pixel declares what it is: tappable things look pressable, choices look choosable, and information sits quietly with no costume.

PART A

Five laws, learned from the research

The person using this app is on their feet, interrupted, and tired. Working memory holds three or four things. Mobile sessions are short and scattered, so a screen gets seconds, not minutes. Everything below is built on that.

Law 1 · One thing first

Every screen opens with exactly one dominant element, at twice the scale of anything else: the answer to "why did I open this screen." Up to three supporting items may follow. Everything else exists as a folded row that says what it holds and how much, and opens on a tap. If two things compete for the top of a screen, the screen is wrong.

Why folding, not hidingDeferred detail measurably speeds people up without costing discovery, because the folded row names its contents and its count. Nothing disappears; it waits. The person in a hallway sees three things. The person with ten minutes opens everything.
The scale jumpHierarchy fails when sizes are close. The hero sits at 21–24sp display; supporting content at 13sp; folded rows at 12sp on sand. The jump is meant to be felt from arm's length.
One primary actionAt most one filled button per screen. If a screen seems to need two, one of them is secondary and dresses accordingly.

Law 2 · Everything declares what it is

The old build's worst trait: identical white boxes, some tappable, some not, some editable, some not. Now there are five costumes and everything on every screen wears exactly one. No bare text links, no mystery cards, no long-press anywhere, all targets 48dp or larger.

Law 3 · Ask one question at a time

Capture stops being a form and becomes a short conversation: one question per stage, big touch targets, progress dots, skip always visible, save always possible. Three stages maximum: who or what, when, and the note. The chips answer most stages in one tap, and the whole flow is designed to finish in under fifteen seconds standing up.

Native before typingVoice is the biggest control on the note stage, not a footnote. The camera is the first-class answer for anything on paper. The right keyboard appears for every field type. Typing is the last resort, not the default.
Context is pre-answeredCapture opened from a person already knows the person; from an incident, the incident. A pre-answered stage shows its answer as a chosen chip and is skipped, one tap to change.
Android, properlyThese are Pixel-proportioned screens: punch-hole camera, gesture bar, bottom navigation with the active-pill indicator, the FAB opening a labeled menu above it rather than a full-screen wall. Spring motion per the existing spec.

Law 4 · Design for year three, not day three

A real notebook holds 1,600 entries, 50 questions, 40 documents, and a dozen lab rounds. Research on long content converges on four tools, and every list in this app gets all four the moment it can grow: a sticky context header so you always know where you are, an index scrubber on the right edge to jump by year and month, search scoped to the section with one tap to widen, and pinning, so the person decides what floats to the top. Folds handle the middle: every closed period, resolved thread, and finished group collapses to a named, counted row.

Adaptive, not fixedScreens lay themselves out by how much they hold. One tracked measure gets a full-screen chart. Four get a hero and rows. Ten get pins, groups, and search. Empty gets a warm invitation. The same screen, four honest sizes, specified per section in Part B.
Pinning is the person's hierarchyAny measure, entry, question set, document, or section can be pinned. Pinned things live in a marked group at the top of their screen, and the Today hero always yields to a pin. The app proposes an order; the person overrules it, permanently, per thing.
Views are a choiceSections with many items carry a view toggle: comfortable list, compact list, and where the content is visual, a grid. The default is chosen per section; the person's choice is remembered per section. Density is theirs, not ours.

Law 5 · The situation shapes the app

The onboarding situation template already decides which sections lead and which fold. It now goes further, so the app a person meets is already shaped like their life. No two situations open the same app.

What the template setsForward and folded sections. The default tracked measures, so a dialysis family sees dialysis sessions on day one and a hospice family never sees growth charts. The capture chips most likely to be needed. What starts pinned. All of it changeable afterward, one screen, no penalty.
Appointments without a cloudA built-in agenda and month view, drawn from the appointments already recorded. No account, no sync, no calendar permission. Each appointment offers "Add to your phone's calendar," which hands that one event to the person's own calendar app and touches nothing else. One-way, user-initiated, nothing read back.
Tests get a homeLab work is not a measurement the family takes; it is a result the family receives, usually several at once, ordered for a reason. So tests live as rounds on the trail: one round per draw or visit, holding its tests, its reason, its documents, and who ordered it. Every test name is a door to that test's own history across every round. Stored once, reachable three ways: by date, by test, by the thread it belongs to.

PART C

How it holds together

Every screen, same skeletonTab chip and title. The one thing, large. Up to three supporting items. Folds for the rest, named and counted. At most one filled action. That skeleton is why screen nineteen will feel familiar the first time it's seen.
The screens not drawn here follow the drawn onesStanding instructions follows Medications. A person follows One entry. A medication history follows the incident spine. The month calendar view follows Appointments with a compact grid. Appointments follows Money's shape: next one as hero, past folded. Standing instructions follows Medications. Chapters and a medication's history follow the incident spine. Ask-next-time follows Care team. A person's page follows One entry. Nothing needs a new pattern.
Depth never exceeds twoTab bar, section, detail. Adding and editing are sheets over where you stand. Folds open in place, not on new screens, unless they hold more than a screenful.
The costume rule is the contractFilled acts, outlined acts smaller, chevroned opens, chipped chooses, sand folds, bare informs. The build enforces it: any tappable element without a costume, or any costumed element without a handler, fails review.
